Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a foundational capability for autonomous systems navigating unknown environments. In practical applications, such as robotics or autonomous driving, these algorithms must operate reliably under highly dynamic and unpredictable conditions. However, many state-of-the-art (SoTA) SLAM solutions suffer from severe performance degradation when exposed to environmental anomalies. To deploy these systems safely, it is critical to rigorously evaluate their performance boundaries and understand their inherent design tradeoffs.

The task of this thesis is to evaluate, benchmark, and profile state-of-the-art SLAM algorithms under challenging environmental conditions. The goal is to systematically analyze their real-time capability and trajectory accuracy to identify which algorithmic paradigms are best suited for adverse scenarios. The student should start with a comprehensive literature review of modern SLAM approaches to make a representative selection of algorithms that highlight distinct design tradeoffs. The evaluation will utilize diverse, demanding datasets that incorporate edge cases such as long-range sessions, intermittent or missing stereo depth, and drastic lighting changes.

The main contribution of this work is to design and implement a containerized benchmarking framework that standardizes the evaluation pipeline. The student will Dockerize the selected SLAM algorithms to ensure reproducible execution environments and develop a unified framework to run identical experiments across all candidates. A specific set of metrics quantifying trajectory errors and real-time execution constraints must be integrated to allow for a direct comparison. Furthermore, the student will conduct an in-depth runtime profiling to isolate which internal components of the algorithms consume the most computational resources. Based on these findings, the student should interpret the results, summarize the architectural tradeoffs, and conclude how specific environmental challenges impact SLAM reliability.
